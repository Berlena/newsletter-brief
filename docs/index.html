The best AI pilots started with the work, not the model

Two fictional dispatches point to the same operating choice. Teams mapped the current handoff, defined exceptions and agreed on stop conditions before trying an assistant. One pilot's first gain was finding three rules that contradicted each other.

Why it matters. A model cannot repair an unclear operating process. The early design work makes a pilot safer to run and gives the team a more useful way to judge it.

Key fact. 120 fabricated tickets exposed three conflicting escalation rules

Today’s through-lineThe useful pattern today is simple: small teams get more from automation when they redesign the handoff before they choose the tool.

A decision log gave a small studio two hours back

An invented 16-person studio replaced two weekly status calls with a shared log that named each open decision, its owner and its review date.

Why it matters. The log did more than reduce meetings. It turned vague updates into visible decisions that someone could close.

Key fact. Reported meeting time moved from five to three hours per person

The invoice test measured the mistakes that mattered

A fictional finance team created four mismatch categories, then tested extraction software on 60 fabricated invoices. Eight cases stayed in human review.

Why it matters. A useful evaluation reflects the team's actual risk. One headline accuracy number would hide whether the tool fails on the cases people care about.

Key fact. Four mismatch categories made vendor comparisons specific
